What this version does and does not do

Solar position follows Meeus (Astronomical Algorithms) with nutation and aberration, checked against the PyEphem library to within a few seconds. Refraction uses the Saemundsson formula scaled by the pressure and temperature you enter, applied to the chosen point on the disk, not to the center.

Not yet in this version: a real terrain skyline from elevation data (the "Skyline elevation" field stands in for it, so set it by hand for now), and a temperature profile or inversion model (the "Refraction multiplier" field lets you explore that by hand). Those are Phase 2 and Phase 3.

Times are rounded to the second but the real uncertainty on any given morning is tens of seconds from the atmosphere alone, which is exactly what the later phases and the calibration log are for.

What counts as sunrise

Horizon

Skyline elevation is the angle of the ridge or building the Sun actually rises behind, measured from level. Phase 2 will fill this in automatically from terrain data for the date's azimuth. Observer height matters when the ground falls away toward the east; leave it at 0 if the skyline is above eye level.

Atmosphere

Using typical Jerusalem values. Press the button to pull live temperature and pressure.

Station pressure is the pressure where you stand, not the sea-level value weather sites show. Jerusalem at 780 m sits near 925 hPa. The multiplier is a hand control for the inversion question: a strong morning inversion over the rift bends light more (try 1.2 to 1.5), dry warm air less.
